*Fictional example. CogniATion, CogniATion Coach, its customers, its people and its competitors are invented for this example. Numbers and quotes are made up.*

**Purpose:** How your company writes, so that every channel sounds like the same company.

Do not fill with guesses – leave blank and the skills will ask.

## Voice

**Voice in three traits**

- **Plain:** short words, no learning jargon.
- **Curious:** asks a real question where others would make a claim.
- **Respectful:** assumes the reader is capable, and never talks down about people who use AI.

**Reading level**

A busy head of learning reading between meetings

**Point of view**

"We" for CogniATion, "you" for the reader

## Rules

**Do**

- Name the work: the memo, the decision, the client call.
- Use numbers only from proof-points.md or the publishable list in company.md.
- Write headlines that say what happens, not how we feel about it.

**Do not**

- No exclamation points.
- No warnings that AI is making people stupid.
- No talk of the future of work.
- No mocking employees for using AI.

**Style details**

CogniATion, never Cogniation or COGNIATION. The Coach on second mention. Sentence case in headlines.

## Examples

**Sounds like us**

Your analysts have answers. Can they defend them?

**Does not sound like us**

Unlock your team's cognitive potential with next-generation AI.

## Visual basics

**Colors, type and logo rules**

Primary deep blue, secondary warm gray, one sans-serif typeface. Logo on plain backgrounds only, never on photos. No images of glowing brains or robots.
