*Fictional example. CogniATion, CogniATion Coach, its customers, its people and its competitors are invented for this example. Numbers and quotes are made up.*

**Purpose:** Which companies you want, which you do not want, and how you split them into segments.

Do not fill with guesses – leave blank and the skills will ask.

## Ideal customer profile

**Company type**

Knowledge-work companies whose staff make judgment calls, and which have someone responsible for learning and development.

**Size**

300 to 5,000 employees, with at least 100 people to train

**Region**

United States, Canada and the United Kingdom

**Situation**

Has rolled out general AI assistants, and leaders worry that junior staff now accept answers without checking them

**Disqualifiers**

Fewer than 100 learners; wants a course library; wants scores on individuals for performance reviews

## Segments

**Segment 1**

**Professional services firms.** Consulting, accounting and law. Buy because junior staff's reasoning reaches clients. Fastest sales cycle. About 45% of customers (internal number).

**Segment 2**

**Software companies.** Buy to build decision-making in new managers. Most price-sensitive. About 35% of customers (internal number).

**Segment 3**

**Financial services firms.** Banks, insurers and asset managers. Buy because analysts' judgment carries risk. Longest security review and highest retention. About 20% of customers (internal number).

**Priority order**

1. Professional services, because it closes fastest. 2. Financial services, because it stays longest. 3. Software companies, served through inbound only.

## Buying process

**Typical sales cycle**

45 to 90 days; longer when security reviews it

**Triggers**

Company-wide AI assistant rollout, a client complaint about poor reasoning, a new head of learning, annual training budget planning
