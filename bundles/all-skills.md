title: Evaluate a campaign
type: skill
function: analytics-and-campaign-evaluation
owner: marketing-analyst
reads: [goals-and-kpis, icp-and-segments, channels-and-tools]
version: 0.1.0
updated: 2026-11-01
summary: Compares a finished campaign's results with its targets, says what worked and sets what to do next.
---

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this after a campaign's time window has closed and you have its numbers. Do not use it for weekly spend decisions on a live paid campaign, which belong to [Adjust paid campaigns](../paid-demand-gen/adjust-paid-campaigns.md), or for credit across all channels, which belongs to [Read attribution](read-attribution.md).

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: MQL, SQL and pipeline definitions, plus KPI baselines for comparison.
- [[context-pack:icp-and-segments]]: ICP criteria for judging lead quality, and the typical sales cycle.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: channels used, paid limits and hard rules.

The marketer pastes into the chat:

- The campaign brief, or its goal, audience, time window, targets and budget.
- Results by channel: spend, leads, MQLs, SQLs and pipeline, with the date pulled.
- Anything that disturbed the results, such as a tracking gap or a changed offer.

## Procedure

1. If the targets or the results are missing, ask for them before drafting. If only part is missing, mark that row "no data". Never reconstruct a target from the results.
2. Fill the results table with one row per target in the brief. Difference is actual minus target, as a number and a percentage.
3. Set each row's status. Use "hit" at or above target and "missed" below it. Use "no data" when the number was not supplied. Use "too early" for SQL, pipeline or won deals when fewer days have passed since the campaign ended than the long end of the typical sales cycle and the number is still below target.
4. Set the verdict from the campaign's one goal metric. "Hit" means the goal metric was hit. "Missed" means it was missed. "Too early" means its status is too early, and the verdict then names the date to read it again: the end date plus the long end of the sales cycle.
5. Calculate cost per MQL for each channel with spend. Show the division. Compare a rate with a baseline only if both use the same definition.
6. Write what worked and what did not. Each bullet needs a number from the table. Under 20 leads in a channel, write "too few to read" and draw no conclusion. That cutoff is a starting rule the marketer can change, not a benchmark. Say "coincided with" rather than "caused" unless the marketer supplied a control group.
7. Write next actions. Each one starts with keep, change or stop. Stop a channel only when it missed its target, is not too early and has enough leads to read. Name an owner from the marketer's input, or write [owner needed].
8. Check the draft against the context pack: terms match the definitions in goals-and-kpis.md, the proposed actions respect the limits in channels-and-tools.md, and lead quality uses the ICP criteria. If a needed field is blank, leave a visible placeholder such as [MQL definition needed] and ask in the last section. Never invent.
9. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section, including any row with no data and the date the numbers were pulled.

## Output format

```
Status: Draft

## Verdict
<Hit, missed or too early, then the goal metric's number. If too early, the date to read it again. Max 40 words.>

## Results against targets
| Metric | Target | Actual | Difference | Status |
<One row per target. Status is hit, missed, too early or no data.>

## Cost by channel
| Channel | Spend | Leads | MQLs | Cost per MQL |
<One row per channel with spend. Write "not supplied" for gaps.>

## What worked
<Up to 3 bullets, max 25 words each, each with a number.>

## What did not work
<Up to 3 bullets, max 25 words each, each with a number.>

## Next actions
| Action | Keep, change or stop | Owner | By when |
<Up to 5 rows.>

## Assumptions and open questions
- Assumption: <what you took as true, and why>
- Estimate: <the number, and how you worked it out>
- Inference: <what you concluded, and from which inputs>
- Unknown: <what you need from the marketer, as a question>
```

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Does the verdict rest on the campaign's one goal metric?
- [ ] Does every target in the brief have a row, with status set by the rule?
- [ ] Are recent SQL and pipeline numbers marked too early rather than missed?
- [ ] Does every worked or did-not-work bullet carry a number from the table?
- [ ] Do MQL, SQL and pipeline match the definitions in goals-and-kpis.md?
- [ ] Is causation claimed only where a control group exists?
- [ ] Does every next action have an owner or a visible placeholder?

**Verdict**

Too early. Goal of $125,000 pipeline stands at $80,000. Read again January 13, 2027.

**Results against targets**

| Metric | Target | Actual | Difference | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MQLs | 40 | 46 | +6, +15% | Hit |
| SQLs | 10 | 6 | -4, -40% | Too early |
| Pipeline | $125,000 | $80,000 | -$45,000, -36% | Too early |

**Cost by channel**

| Channel | Spend | Leads | MQLs | Cost per MQL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Analyst reasoning webinar | $4,500 | 88 | 46 | $98 |

**What worked**

- Webinar topic drew 46 MQLs against a target of 40.

**What did not work**

- 6 SQLs from 46 MQLs is 13% so far, against the 26% baseline, with the sales cycle still open.

**Next actions**

| Action | Keep, change or stop | Owner | By when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep the analyst reasoning topic for the next webinar | Keep | Marketer | January 2027 |
| Read pipeline again | Keep | Marketer | January 13, 2027 |

**Assumptions and open questions**

- Estimate: $4,500 / 46 = $98. Reread date is the end date plus 90 days.
- Inference: too early, from 17 days elapsed against the 45 to 90 day sales cycle.
- Unknown: How many of the 42 leads short of MQL missed the ICP, such as having fewer than 100 learners?
- Unknown: How many of the 46 MQLs has sales called so far?

---
title: Read attribution
type: skill
function: analytics-and-campaign-evaluation
owner: marketing-analyst
reads: [goals-and-kpis, icp-and-segments, personas, channels-and-tools]
version: 0.1.0
updated: 2026-11-01
summary: Reads first and last touch data side by side and says what can and cannot be concluded from it.
---

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this when you have pipeline or revenue by channel and need to know how far the credit can be trusted. Do not use it to judge one campaign against its targets, which belongs to [Evaluate a campaign](evaluate-campaign.md), or to split next period's money, which belongs to [Allocate the budget](../budget-and-planning/allocate-budget.md).

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: the attribution model and the definition of pipeline.
- [[context-pack:icp-and-segments]]: the typical sales cycle, for judging how mature the data is.
- [[context-pack:personas]]: where buyers get information, for naming what tracking cannot see.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: the channels in use and the tools that record touches.

The marketer pastes into the chat:

- A CRM export or summary with, for each channel, first touch pipeline and opportunities and last touch pipeline and opportunities.
- The period covered and the date the export was pulled.
- Any tracking change in the period, and when each campaign in the data started.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the model in goals-and-kpis.md. If it is blank, ask. If the export holds only one of the two touches, say the comparison cannot be made, write "not supplied" in the missing column and limit conclusions to the touch supplied.
2. Fill the credit table with one row per channel, plus a row for direct or unknown and a total row. Show dollars and opportunities for each touch.
3. Compare the two totals. If they differ, report the gap and ask why, because the columns do not then cover the same deals. Never add first and last touch together, since a deal with both touches would count twice.
4. Label each channel. Calculate its share of first touch dollars and its share of last touch dollars. "Starter" means first touch share is at least 1.5 times last touch share. "Closer" means the reverse. "Both" means anything in between. "Too few" means fewer than 5 opportunities on both touches, and it overrides the other labels. These cutoffs and the 25% in step 5 are starting rules the marketer can change, not benchmarks.
5. Apply two data warnings. If direct or unknown is above 25% of either total, label that row "warning" and say channel conclusions are weak. If a campaign started fewer days before the pull date than the short end of the sales cycle, mark it "still maturing" and draw no conclusion from it.
6. Write what can be concluded. Use only statements the data shows, in the form "[channel] was first touch on [number] opportunities worth [amount]". Label anything further as an inference and name its inputs.
7. Write what cannot be concluded. Always include these points: the data does not show that a channel caused pipeline, or whether a deal would have happened anyway, and it says nothing about touches between the first and the last. Add return on spend unless spend was supplied.
8. Name the blind spots. List each information source from personas.md that tracking cannot record, such as peers or professional associations, and say that credit for those conversations lands on a later touch.
9. Write up to 3 actions, each framed as a test with the number to watch and when to read it. Never recommend cutting a channel from a single touch, or from a channel labeled too few or still maturing.
10. Check the draft against the context pack: pipeline and model wording match goals-and-kpis.md, and channel names match channels-and-tools.md. If a needed field is blank, leave a visible placeholder such as [pull date needed] and ask in the last section. Never invent.
11. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section.

## Output format

```
Status: Draft

## Bottom line
<What the data supports and how far to trust it. Max 40 words.>

## Credit by channel
| Channel | First touch | Last touch | Label |
<Dollars and opportunities in each touch column. Include direct or unknown and a total row. Label is starter, closer, both, too few, still maturing or warning.>

## What can be concluded
<Up to 4 bullets, max 25 words each.>

## What cannot be concluded
<Up to 4 bullets, max 25 words each.>

## Blind spots
<Up to 3 bullets, max 20 words each.>

## Tests to run
| Test | Number to watch | Read on |
<Up to 3 rows.>

## Assumptions and open questions
- Assumption: <what you took as true, and why>
- Estimate: <the number, and how you worked it out>
- Inference: <what you concluded, and from which inputs>
- Unknown: <what you need from the marketer, as a question>
```

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Are first and last touch shown side by side and never summed?
- [ ] Do the two totals match, or is the gap reported?
- [ ] Does every channel label follow the share rule?
- [ ] Is direct or unknown shown as its own row, with the warning applied?
- [ ] Is causation left out of the conclusions?
- [ ] Are the blind spots taken from personas.md?
- [ ] Is every action framed as a test, with no cut based on one touch?

## Example (fictional company)

Status: Draft

Assumption: the $900,000 total is CogniATion's Q3 2026 pipeline baseline. The split by channel is illustrative, as if pasted by the marketer.

**Bottom line**

Webinars and email close deals that other channels start, but unknown touches are above 25% of first touch, so treat channel conclusions as weak.

**Credit by channel**

| Channel | First touch | Last touch | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search ads | $252,000 (19) | $180,000 (14) | Both |
| Organic search | $198,000 (15) | $126,000 (9) | Starter |
| Social network ads | $108,000 (8) | $54,000 (4) | Starter |
| Webinars | $72,000 (5) | $216,000 (16) | Closer |
| Email | $27,000 (2) | $135,000 (10) | Closer |
| Direct or unknown | $243,000 (19) | $189,000 (15) | Warning |
| Total | $900,000 (68) | $900,000 (68) | |

**What can be concluded**

- Webinars were last touch on 16 opportunities worth $216,000.

**What cannot be concluded**

- Whether webinars caused those deals, or whether they would have happened anyway.

**Blind spots**

- Peers in other companies and learning and development associations leave no tracked touch.

**Tests to run**

| Test | Number to watch | Read on |
|---|---|---|
| Invite search ad leads to a webinar for heads of learning | MQL to SQL rate, baseline 26% | End of Q1 2027 |

**Assumptions and open questions**

- Estimate: 68 opportunities is 260 MQLs times the 26% rate, both Q3 2026 baselines.
- Unknown: How much of direct or unknown comes from untagged links or the video channel?

title: Specify a dashboard
type: skill

version: 0.1.0
updated: 2026-11-01
summary: Writes a dashboard spec with metrics, definitions, sources and views, which the marketer builds in their own tool.
---

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this before building or rebuilding a marketing dashboard, so the definitions are settled first. Do not use it to read the numbers themselves, which belongs to [Write the monthly report](write-monthly-report.md), or to fix link tagging, which belongs to [Set tracking conventions](../marketing-ops/set-tracking-conventions.md).

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: KPIs, definitions, baselines, targets and review cadence.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: which tool does which job, and who has access.
- [[context-pack:company]]: which numbers are internal only and which must never appear.

The marketer pastes into the chat:

- Who will look at the dashboard, and the decision each person makes from it.
- The tool it will be built in.
- Field names or export column headers from each source tool, if available.

## Procedure

1. Write the purpose: one audience and the decisions it supports. If the audiences need different numbers, give each its own view rather than a separate metric list.
2. List metrics. Start with every KPI in goals-and-kpis.md. Add another metric only if the marketer names a decision it changes. Cap the list at 12, and record what you cut.
3. Give each metric a definition, copied from goals-and-kpis.md where one exists. If none exists, write a proposed definition and label it an assumption. Never change a definition silently.
4. Give each metric a source, using the tool by job from channels-and-tools.md: pipeline and SQLs from the CRM, visits from the web analytics tool, and so on. If the marketer supplied field names, name the field. Otherwise write [field to confirm]. If a metric needs data from two tools, mark it "manual join" and name the shared field.
5. Set the refresh from the KPI's review cadence. A KPI reviewed weekly refreshes weekly, and so on.
6. Set the target from goals-and-kpis.md. If a KPI has only a baseline, show the baseline and write "no target set". Do not make one up.
7. Lay out the views. Make one view per review cadence that has KPIs, in order: weekly, monthly, quarterly. Choose the display by this rule: a goal shows as one number against target and pace, a trend shows as a line over time, and a comparison across channels shows as a table or bars.
8. Set access. A number marked internal only in company.md goes only in views for people inside the company. A number marked never to be mentioned stays off the dashboard. Flag any metric whose source tool the viewer cannot open, as listed in channels-and-tools.md.
9. Write the build check: recalculate each KPI for the last closed period and compare it with its baseline in goals-and-kpis.md. A mismatch means the definition or the source is wrong, so fix that before anyone reads the dashboard.
10. Check the draft against the context pack: every KPI is present, definitions are unchanged and tool jobs match. If a needed field is blank, leave a visible placeholder such as [target needed] and ask in the last section. Never invent.
11. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section, including each unconfirmed field.

## Output format

The marketer builds the dashboard from it.

```
Status: Draft

## Purpose
<Audience and the decisions it supports. Max 40 words.>

## Metrics
| Metric | Definition | Source | Refresh | Target or baseline |
<Up to 12 rows. Definition max 20 words.>

## Views
<One bullet per view: name, cadence, metrics in order, display for each. Max 40 words each.>

## Access
<Up to 4 bullets, max 25 words each.>

## Build check
<Up to 5 numbered steps, max 25 words each.>

## Assumptions and open questions
- Assumption: <what you took as true, and why>
- Estimate: <the number, and how you worked it out>
- Inference: <what you concluded, and from which inputs>
- Unknown: <what you need from the marketer, as a question>
```

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Is every KPI in goals-and-kpis.md on the list?
- [ ] Are the definitions copied without silent changes?
- [ ] Does every metric have a source, with unconfirmed fields marked?
- [ ] Does each added metric have a named decision behind it?
- [ ] Does each view match a review cadence?
- [ ] Are internal-only numbers limited and never-mention numbers absent?
- [ ] Does the build check reproduce a known baseline?

title: Write the monthly report
type: skill
function: analytics-and-campaign-evaluation
owner: marketing-analyst
reads: [goals-and-kpis, channels-and-tools, company]
version: 0.1.0
updated: 2026-11-01
summary: Reports one closed month against goals-and-kpis.md, with short commentary and the decisions it calls for.
---

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this once a month, after the month has closed and you can pull its numbers. Do not use it to judge one campaign, which belongs to [Evaluate a campaign](evaluate-campaign.md), or to set the coming week's work, which belongs to [Set weekly priorities](../budget-and-planning/set-weekly-priorities.md).

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: quarterly goals, definitions, KPI baselines and review cadence.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: planned paid spend by channel.
- [[context-pack:company]]: which numbers are publishable, internal only or never to be mentioned.

The marketer pastes into the chat:

- The month being reported, and who will read the report.
- The month's actual for each KPI, plus quarter-to-date totals for each goal.
- Actual spend by channel, and planned spend if it differs from the context pack.
- Events that moved the numbers, such as a tracking gap, a launch or a staffing change.

## Procedure

1. Write one KPI row for every KPI in goals-and-kpis.md. Report KPIs reviewed weekly or monthly as the month's figure. Report a KPI reviewed quarterly only in the last month of a quarter, and otherwise write "next quarterly review". If an actual is missing, write [actual needed]. Never estimate an actual.
2. Convert baselines to a monthly figure. Divide a count or dollar baseline from a full quarter by 3 and label it an estimate. Compare rates directly, without dividing.
3. Write one goal row for every quarterly goal. Pace to date is the goal multiplied by months elapsed, divided by 3. For a dated goal that is not a number, such as a campaign publish date, use the marketer's status or write [status needed].
4. Set each numeric goal's status from actual divided by pace. "On pace" is 100% or more, "at risk" is 80% to 99%, and "off pace" is below 80%. These cutoffs and the 10% in step 7 are starting rules the marketer can change, not benchmarks.
5. Fill the spend table. Difference is actual minus planned.
6. Write the headline last, and put it first in the output. It names the goal furthest behind pace and the number that shows it. If every goal is on pace, it names the goal with the smallest margin.
7. Write commentary only for goals at risk or off pace, and for KPIs that differ from their monthly baseline by more than 10%. Explain a change only from what the marketer supplied. Label any explanation you worked out yourself as an inference, and otherwise write [reason not supplied].
8. Write up to 3 decisions the numbers call for. Each names the choice, your recommendation and the date it must be made by. Write "None this month" if no goal is at risk or off pace and no spend line is over plan.
9. Check the draft against the context pack: definitions match goals-and-kpis.md, and no number marked "never mention" in company.md appears. Mark the report "Internal" at the top if it holds any internal-only number. If it will go outside the company, use only publishable numbers. If a needed field is blank, leave a visible placeholder and ask in the last section. Never invent.
10. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section, including each baseline you divided and the date the numbers were pulled.

## Output format

```
Status: Draft

## Headline
<Audience marking, month, the goal furthest behind pace and its number. Max 50 words.>

## Goals
| Goal | Target | To date | Pace to date | Status |
<One row per quarterly goal. Status is on pace, at risk or off pace.>

## KPIs
| KPI | Month actual | Monthly baseline | Difference |
<One row per KPI in goals-and-kpis.md.>

## Spend
| Channel | Planned | Actual | Difference |
<One row per paid channel, then a total.>

## Commentary
<Up to 4 bullets, max 30 words each.>

## Decisions needed
| Decision | Recommendation | Decide by |
<Up to 3 rows, or "None this month".>

## Assumptions and open questions
- Assumption: <what you took as true, and why>
- Estimate: <the number, and how you worked it out>
- Inference: <what you concluded, and from which inputs>
- Unknown: <what you need from the marketer, as a question>
```

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Does every KPI and quarterly goal in goals-and-kpis.md have a row?
- [ ] Does the headline name the goal furthest behind pace, with its number?
- [ ] Is every status set by the pace rule rather than by feel?
- [ ] Is every divided baseline labeled an estimate?
- [ ] Is every explanation either supplied by the marketer or labeled an inference?
- [ ] Is every number's audience correct under company.md?
- [ ] Does each decision have a recommendation and a date?

## Example (fictional company)

Status: Draft

Assumption: all of CogniATion's January 2027 actuals are illustrative, as if pasted by the marketer. Marked Internal because company.md does not list pipeline or SQLs as publishable.

**Headline**

Internal. January 2027: pipeline is the goal furthest behind, at $352,000 against a pace of $400,000.

**Goals**

| SQLs | 90 | 27 | 30 | At risk (90%) |
| Professional services campaign | February 15, 2027 | [status needed] | n/a | [status needed] |

**KPIs**

| KPI | Month actual | Monthly baseline | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| MQLs | 97 | 87 | +10 |
| MQL to SQL rate | 28% | 26% | +2 points |

**Spend**

| Channel | Planned | Actual | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|

title: Codify the brand voice
type: skill
function: brand-and-design-briefs
owner: brand-and-creative-director
reads: [company, personas, messaging, proof-points, brand-voice]
version: 0.1.0
updated: 2026-11-01
summary: Builds or updates brand-voice.md from writing samples the marketer pastes, with evidence for every rule.
---

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this when brand-voice.md is blank, or when the company's writing has changed and the file no longer describes it. Do not use it to check one piece of writing against the voice; use [Review any output](../marketing-ops/review-any-output.md) instead. Do not use it to change what the company claims; use [Build a messaging framework](../positioning-and-messaging/build-messaging-framework.md) instead.

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:brand-voice]]: the current file, if filled, to compare against the samples.
- [[context-pack:company]]: public name.
- [[context-pack:personas]]: who the writing is for, to set the reading level.
- [[context-pack:messaging]]: words to use and words to avoid, so that the voice does not contradict them.
- [[context-pack:proof-points]]: which quotes belong to customers, so that they are not counted as the company's voice.

The marketer supplies in the prompt: at least five writing samples the company has published or would publish, ideally from more than one channel, each marked with its channel and date. Add any lines the company would never publish, and any visual rules.

## Procedure

10. Flag approvals. For each line above the budget threshold in channels-and-tools.md, name the approver and lead time.
11. Check the draft against the context pack: total matches the budget, hard rules respected, every dated commitment funded or flagged as unfunded.

9. Set one or two launch measures from goals-and-kpis.md, with baselines and a date to read them. Leave targets without a basis as placeholders.
10. Write what happens if the release slips: which assets hold, and who tells sales.

    - Fix first: there is at least one blocker or an unknown, and all can be fixed before the launch date.
    - Go: no blockers and no unknowns. Fix items can remain, listed with owners.

8. Compare spend to date with the monthly budget per channel and say whether it is on pace, over or under.
9. Write the steps the marketer takes in the ad tool, in order. Say nothing as if it were already done.
10. Check the decisions against the context pack: limits, forbidden ad types, words to avoid, budget, approval thresholds. Fix what contradicts it.

- [[context-pack:proof-points]] – evidence and permission status

## Procedure

1. Take the pasted text, or messaging.md if nothing is pasted. If the text and messaging.md are both blank, ask for the text. Do not review from memory.
2. Set the intended use from the prompt. If it is missing, treat the text as public, the strictest case, and record that as an assumption.
3. Split the text into numbered claims. A claim is any statement a buyer could check or dispute. Quote each one exactly, at most 20 words.
4. Run the accuracy test on each claim: does product.md support it, and does it stay clear of "What it is not", "Known weak spots" and roadmap items? A claim stronger than the fact fails. A claim with no fact in product.md is marked unknown, not false.
5. Run the proof test: is there an item in proof-points.md, or a publishable number in company.md, that backs this exact claim, with a permission status that allows the intended use? Major: a claim stronger than the fact, no proof, proof that measures something else, or "true of all" on a pillar. Minor: wording, or any other differentiation result short of "cannot tell".
9. Set the verdict. Any blocker: "Do not use". No blocker and at least one major: "Fix first". Otherwise: "Usable".
10. For each finding, name the evidence by file and field, and give a fix direction in 20 words at most. Do not rewrite the claim unless the marketer asks.
11. List the claims that passed all tests, so the marketer knows what to keep.
12. Check the review itself against the context pack: every finding must cite a real field, and nothing you say about a competitor can go beyond competitors.md.
13. List everything you assumed, inferred or could not verify in the last section, each with its label.

Use this when you have no positioning statement, enter a new segment, or reach the quarterly positioning review. Do not use it to write pillars and persona messages; use [Build a messaging framework](build-messaging-framework.md) instead. ## Inputs

From the context pack:

- [[context-pack:product]] – category, features, limits, weak spots
- [[context-pack:icp-and-segments]] – priority order, situation, triggers
- [[context-pack:personas]] – the champion and their measures
- [[context-pack:messaging]] – the current statement and words to avoid
- [[context-pack:competitors]] – alternatives, the status quo, dated facts
- [[context-pack:proof-points]] – evidence and permission status

The marketer supplies in the prompt: the target segment, if it is not the first priority, and any new evidence, pasted with source and date.

## Procedure

1. Read the Inputs files. If the category, the ICP or the competitors are blank, ask all the questions together before drafting. If the marketer wants a draft anyway, leave a visible placeholder such as [category needed]. Never invent.
2. Pick one target. Use the segment the marketer names, otherwise the first in the priority order in icp-and-segments.md. If there is no priority order, ask. Use the persona marked as the champion in personas.md, or ask if none is marked.
3. List the alternatives: every competitor in competitors.md plus the status quo. A pasted competitor claim with no source and date stays out and is listed as unknown.
4. For each alternative, write what the product does that the alternative does not. Keep a difference only if product.md lists the capability, competitors.md backs the gap with "Where they lose" or a dated fact, and the difference does not touch "What it is not" or "Known weak spots".
5. Turn each difference into one value tied to what the target persona is measured on. Drop differences that reach no measure and keep at most 4 values.
6. Score each value out of 3, one point each: it has proof with status Public in proof-points.md, it answers a situation or trigger in icp-and-segments.md, and competitors.md records no alternative that can also claim it. Pending and None items count as no proof. So does proof found only in messaging.md, which also goes under unknowns.
7. Choose the lead value: the highest score. Break a tie with the value closest to the first item the persona is measured on. If the top score is 1, say under "What would break it" that the position is weak.
8. Take the category from product.md. Propose another only if the marketer asks.
9. Write the statement in the exact form shown in the template. The reason to believe must be a fact from product.md. Use at most two "Unlike" sentences, choosing the alternatives whose differences rest on dated facts first.
10. Write the one-sentence description with no competitor names. Reuse the current one if it still fits the lead value.
11. Fill in the reasoning table and name the segments and buyer types the statement gives up.

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this in the first hours of an incident, when people are asking and the facts are incomplete: an outage, a data problem, a safety event, a public complaint. Do not use it once the facts are known and you are announcing a fix or a change; use [Write a press release](write-press-release.md) instead. It does not replace a lawyer when step 2 says to call one.

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:company]]: public name, who may speak, press contact.
- [[context-pack:product]]: what the product does and is not, so the statement does not overclaim.
- [[context-pack:messaging]]: words to avoid, including any promise the incident now contradicts.
- [[context-pack:brand-voice]]: tone and things never to say.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: approvers and owned channels.

The marketer supplies in the prompt: what happened as far as confirmed, who confirmed it, when it started, who is affected, what is being done, who is asking, and when the next update can realistically happen.

## Procedure

1. Sort every fact from the prompt into two lists: confirmed (with who confirmed it) and not yet known. Anything without a named source goes in not yet known. If the prompt does not say what is confirmed, ask before drafting.
2. Run the escalation check. If the incident involves injury or death, personal or customer data, a regulator, police, a legal threat, or a claim about product safety, write "Stop: get a lawyer and the CEO before anything goes out" at the top, and still draft for their review.
3. Set the mode. Choose "reactive, send only when asked" when few customers are affected and no one outside has noticed. Choose "proactive" when customers are affected or a public report exists. In proactive mode, affected customers hear before anyone else.
4. Write the statement in four parts, in this order: what we know, who is affected as far as we know, what we are doing, when we will next update. Use only the confirmed list.
5. Do not state a cause, a fix time, an amount of data or a number of customers unless it is on the confirmed list. Do not say "no customer data was affected" unless a named person has checked. Do not blame a person, a supplier or a customer.
6. Write without marketing: no proof points, no customer names, no product benefits and no claims about reliability. A product feature that might limit the effect, such as a backup or offline mode, goes in only if someone has confirmed it worked during this incident.
7. Set the next update time from the prompt. If there is none, leave [next update time needed]. Never promise one the marketer did not give.
8. Write the customer note: the same facts, plus what the customer should do now. If nothing, say so.
9. Write 5 to 7 Q&A items, covering the hardest questions first: what happened, who is affected, is data safe, whose fault is it, when will it be fixed. Where the honest answer is unknown, answer "We do not know yet" and say when that will change.
10. Fill the escalation table from company.md and channels-and-tools.md. If a needed field is blank, or no approver is listed for incidents, or the usual approval time is too slow, leave a visible placeholder in square brackets and ask in the last section. Never invent.
11. Check the draft against the context pack: every word against words to avoid and brand-voice.md, and every statement against the confirmed list. Remove anything that fails.
12. List everything you assumed, and everything not yet known, in the last section.

<60 words maximum. Same facts, plus what to do now>

<5 to 7 pairs, hardest first>

## Do not say
- <Up to 5 tempting lines to avoid, each with the reason>

|---|---|---|
| Approves the statement | <name or placeholder> | <before release> |
| Speaks to press | <name or placeholder> | <when> |
| Handles press inquiries | <contact> | <when> |

- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: the organic KPI and its baseline.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: who can change what on the site.

The marketer pastes at least one export. The search console export needs page, query if available, clicks, impressions, CTR and average position, for two equal periods with dates. The crawl export needs URL, status code, indexability, canonical, title, meta description, inlinks and click depth. Also useful: dates of site changes and a list of the pages that matter most.

## Procedure

1. Inventory the data: file, date range, row count and columns. If a row count is a round limit, such as exactly 1,000, flag it as possibly truncated. If no export is pasted, stop and ask. Any check whose columns are missing goes under Not checked.
2. Tag each URL as ICP page or Other. It is an ICP page if the marketer listed it, or if its topic matches a segment or the product. Label topic matches as an inference. If icp-and-segments.md is blank, write [ICP needed] and ask in the last section.
3. Run these checks in order. Every finding names URLs and quotes numbers from the export. The cutoffs are starting rules the marketer can change, not benchmarks.
   - Blocked: status 4xx or 5xx, noindex, or a canonical pointing elsewhere.
   - Lost ground: clicks down 25% or more between periods, from at least 20 clicks.
   - Near win: average position 4 to 15, with impressions in the top quarter of the export.

   - Competing pages: two or more URLs each holding at least 10% of a query's impressions.
   - Structure: an ICP page with 2 inlinks or fewer, or click depth over 3.
   - Off target: impressions driven by queries for something product.md says the product is not.
4. Rate impact. High: an ICP page with a Blocked, Lost ground or Near win finding. Medium: an ICP page with any other finding, or an Other page that is Blocked. Low: everything else.
5. Rate effort from who can make the change, using channels-and-tools.md. Low: one page, in the CMS, by the marketer. Medium: a page rewrite. High: engineering, or a change across the site.
6. Rank fixes in this order: High impact and Low effort, High and Medium, Medium and Low, High and High, Medium and Medium, then the rest. Keep the top 10. The rest go under Parked with a reason.
7. Write each fix: what to change, on which URL, who does it, and the number to check in next month's export. For a title or description problem, draft the replacement within the limits and the brand voice.
8. State causes only as far as the data shows. Label a cause as an inference and name the inputs, or write "cause unknown". Do not mention search engine updates or competitor moves unless the marketer supplied them.
9. Compare with the organic KPI only if the marketer pasted the same measure. Clicks in a search console export are not the same as visits in a web analytics tool. If the KPI is blank, write [organic KPI needed].
10. Check the draft against the context pack: replacement titles use no word to avoid, promise only what product.md supports, and owners match channels-and-tools.md.
11. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section.

- [[context-pack:competitors]]: competitor names and dated facts, for comparison pages.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: how often the marketer publishes.

The marketer pastes a keyword export (keyword, monthly volume, difficulty if available, plus the export's source and date) and a list of existing pages (URL and title).

## Procedure

4. Set one intent per keyword:
   - Buy: software, pricing, vendor, demo, "best" plus the category.
   - Compare: "vs", "alternative", a competitor's name.
   - Solve: how to, checklist, template, exercise.
   - Learn: what is, meaning, definition.
   If the wording fits two, take the one closer to buying.

   - Hold: a Compare cluster names a competitor and competitors.md has no dated fact about it.
8. Set the tier. Tier 1: Buy or Compare in the first or second priority segment, or All. Tier 2: Solve in those segments, or Buy or Compare in any other segment. Tier 3: everything else. Within a tier, sort by volume, high to low, with unknown last. Break ties by lower difficulty.
9. Pick the next three briefs: the first three rows that are not Hold. Say how long they take at the publishing rate in channels-and-tools.md, labeled as an inference.
10. Check the draft against the context pack: each kept cluster needs a page that product.md and messaging.md can support, with no roadmap item and no word to avoid in its name or slug. Exclude any that fail.

**Draft only.** The model does not know your market. It produces a draft, plan, analysis or review, and you, the marketer, decide what to use.

## When to use

Use this when you have chosen one topic and one page and need a brief the content lead can write from. Do not use it to choose topics; use [Build a keyword map](build-keyword-map.md) instead. To write the page itself, use [Write a web page](../content/write-web-page.md) or [Write a blog post](../content/write-blog-post.md).

## Inputs

- [[context-pack:product]]: features and limits.
- [[context-pack:icp-and-segments]]: the segment, its buying reason and its triggers.
- [[context-pack:personas]]: the reader's daily problem and objections.
- [[context-pack:messaging]]: pillars, words to use and avoid.
- [[context-pack:competitors]]: dated facts, if the page compares.
- [[context-pack:proof-points]]: evidence and permission status.
- [[context-pack:brand-voice]]: rules for title and description.
- [[context-pack:goals-and-kpis]]: the conversion that counts, current campaigns.
- [[context-pack:channels-and-tools]]: who approves what.

The marketer supplies the topic and primary keyword. The skill works only from what is pasted, so the marketer also pastes the titles and headings of the top results, the volume, and any URLs available for internal links. Without them, those fields say unknown.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the scope is one topic and one page. If the request holds two intents, brief the one closer to buying and name the other under the last section.
2. Set the intent: Buy, Compare, Solve or Learn. If search results are pasted, use the intent most of them serve. If not, infer it from the keyword's wording and label it as an inference.
3. Set the page type by intent: Buy gets a product or solution page, Compare a comparison page, Solve a guide or blog post, Learn a blog post.
4. Choose one reader: the persona whose daily problem the keyword addresses. If two fit, take the one closer to the buying decision for that segment. If personas.md is blank, ask before drafting.
5. Write the angle in one sentence: what this page says that the pasted results do not, tied to one pillar. With no results pasted, base it on the pillar and mark the gap as unknown.
6. Write the title tag (60 characters at most), meta description (155 at most), H1 and slug. The primary keyword goes in the title, H1 and slug. Follow brand-voice.md.
7. Write the outline: 4 to 8 H2 headings. The first answers the search query directly. For each, state the question it must answer, the keyword it carries if any, and the proof it uses.
8. Choose proof from proof-points.md. A public page uses only items marked public. Leave out sales only, internal only, pending and none, and do not name those customers in the brief. Use a claim from messaging.md that names a customer only if proof-points.md shows permission for that claim. If a section has no usable proof, write [proof needed].
9. Write the do-not-say list: limits from product.md, the words to avoid, and any roadmap item. A Compare page names a competitor only with a dated fact from competitors.md. Any pricing mention goes to the approver in channels-and-tools.md.
10. Choose the page's one action. Buy and Compare pages ask for a demo or the pilot in product.md. Solve and Learn pages offer the next page to read, plus a smaller demo link. Add 3 to 5 internal links from the pasted URLs only, or write [internal link needed: topic].
11. Set the length from the median of the pasted results, or write [length needed]. Name the handoff: the content lead, the skill to run and the approver.
12. Check the draft against the context pack: every feature, number, name and word. Fix anything that contradicts it.
13. List every assumption, estimate, inference and unknown in the last section.
