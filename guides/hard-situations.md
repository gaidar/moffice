These are the three conversations most likely to catch a marketer alone. For each, the rule is the same: find out where the problem sits, say what you know and what you do not, and name the day you will know more.

## Who it is for

A marketer on their own who is facing one of these this week, with nobody senior to ask. Go straight to the one you need.

## What you need

- Your office set up, or the plain-chat fallback from your setup guide.
- Access to your CRM, or someone who can export from it.
- Last quarter's MQLs, SQLs and pipeline value, if you have them.
- About 30 minutes for the first step of each, and a few days to get real answers. These are estimates.

## Steps

### Sales says the leads are bad

Find out which leads and why, before anyone discusses whose fault it is.

1. Pull the last 30 MQLs that sales rejected, with the reason for each. If no reason was recorded, ask the sales lead for one per lead. That gap is a finding too.
2. Sort them by reason: not ICP, wrong person, no contact, duplicate, not ready.
3. Mark which ones you could have stopped, and which you cannot explain yet.
4. Ask the sales lead for 30 minutes to go through the list together:

   ```
   I pulled the last 30 leads you rejected and sorted them by reason. Some look like mine to fix. Can we go through them, so I stop sending you the wrong ones?
   ```

   In the meeting, ask "Which of these should never have reached you?" and "What did a good one look like last month?" Do not defend the list.
5. Run [Define the lead handoff](../skills/marketing-ops/define-lead-handoff.md), and paste in what you both agreed.

**CogniATion example, with illustrative counts.** Tom Ferrant, head of sales, says the leads are bad. Of the last 30 rejected: 14 not ICP, 9 not ready, 5 no contact, 2 wrong person. The 14 had fewer than 100 learners, a disqualifier the marketer can filter on before handoff. For the 5, Tom checks how many attempts were made. Now they share two specific fixes, not an argument about quality.

### The CEO asks why pipeline is down

Answer the same day with what you know, and name the day you will know why.

1. Run [Diagnose a pipeline gap](../skills/analytics-and-campaign-evaluation/diagnose-pipeline-gap.md). If you have no target, tell it to use last quarter's numbers as the target line.
2. Read the split. It shows where the gap sits (volume, conversion or deal size), not why.
3. Say this today:

   ```
   Pipeline is down [amount] on last quarter, mostly in [volume, conversion or deal size]. I do not know why yet. I am asking two reps and reading five lost deals, and I will tell you by [day]. I am not changing spend until then.
   ```

4. Find out why. Ask two reps, "What changed in the last 90 days, in who you talk to and what they say?" Read five lost deals, or five rejected leads if conversion is the gap, and note what each one says.
5. Go back on that day with what you heard, how many times you heard it, and the skill you will run next.

**CogniATion example, with illustrative numbers.** As if its pack had no target: Q4 closes at $675,000, against Q3's $900,000. MQLs 250 against 260, conversion 20% against 26%. The split: volume -$34,600, conversion -$199,700, deal size +$9,300. The marketer tells Marta Ilves, CEO, "Down $225,000, almost all conversion, and I will know why by Friday."

### You have no goals yet

Goals are the CEO's to choose. Your job is to bring options with the arithmetic done, so that choosing takes minutes.

1. Run [Propose quarterly goals](../skills/budget-and-planning/propose-quarterly-goals.md), with whatever numbers you have.
2. Send the options a day ahead, and book 30 minutes with your CEO. Use this script:

   ```
   I have three goal options for next quarter, worked out from last quarter's numbers. They are options, not forecasts.
   What does the company most need from marketing this quarter?
   Which option do you want to hold me to?
   What budget can I plan against?
   What should I drop to make room for it?
   ```

3. Write the choice into the goals-and-kpis section of `my-context-pack.md`, then follow [the quarterly playbook](../playbooks/quarterly.md).
4. If the CEO will not choose, do not wait. Send this in writing, and mark the goal "proposed, not agreed" in your file:

   ```
   I will plan against the middle option unless you tell me otherwise by [day]. I will report against it every month, and we can change it at any point.
   ```

**CogniATion example, with illustrative options.** As if its goals were blank, with only Q3's $900,000 pipeline and 26% MQL to SQL. The options, from [Propose quarterly goals](../skills/budget-and-planning/propose-quarterly-goals.md): hold at $900,000; grow to about $1.03 million; stretch to about $1.19 million, which is 90 SQLs from 300 MQLs at 30%. Marta Ilves picks stretch, rounded to $1.2 million, with a $70,000 budget, which is what its [goals file](../context-pack/example-cogniation/goals-and-kpis.md) records. If the rate stays at 26%, 90 SQLs need about 346 MQLs, a third more than Q3's 260.

## What success looks like

The sales lead agrees to specific rules, and you both know which rejections belong to whom. The CEO heard the bad news from you first, knows where the gap sits and knows the day you will know why. Your quarter has a goal the CEO chose or, at the least, one the CEO has seen in writing. Everything you repeated as a cause came from a person or a deal, not from the model. To test what the model gives you in these situations, see [Judge an output](judge-an-output.md).

## Troubleshooting

- **The CRM has no rejection reasons.** Sit with the sales lead, or the person who calls the leads, and take 10 minutes to mark them from memory. Label the result as their recollection, then add the reason field through Define the lead handoff.
- **You have fewer than 30 rejected MQLs.** Use all you have, and say the sample is small.
- **The sales lead will not meet.** Send the sorted list, and ask one question: "Which reason here is wrong?" Bring in your CEO only after that.
- **You have no numbers at all for the split.** Say so, and run [Set tracking conventions](../skills/marketing-ops/set-tracking-conventions.md) so that next quarter has some. Meanwhile, the two reps and five lost deals still work.
- **The CEO wants the reason today.** Repeat where the gap sits and the day you will know why. Do not offer a guess, because it will be repeated as fact.
- **The model states a cause.** Treat it as an Inference until a person or a deal record confirms it.
- **The CEO picks a goal the numbers do not support.** Show the arithmetic once: "That needs [number] MQLs, and we made [number] last quarter. What should change to make that possible?" Then plan against the goal and report the gap every month.
