Start with the CEO on day 1, spread five more conversations across the week, and ship one thing sales can use. Strategy waits for weeks 2 to 4, when you know what the CEO, sales and customers say. Skip what you already have.

## Before you begin

No sales team yet, and a founder still sells? Use [Starting from zero](from-zero.md) instead, because this playbook needs people you do not have.

Run the fast first pass from [Start here](../guides/start-here.md), so you have one my-context-pack.md. Blanks are acceptable. Book about 45 minutes with the CEO for day 1.

**The one-pager does not wait for the conversations.** It gets drafted and reviewed before they are finished, on purpose. Sales reacts better to a page than to a blank question, and you will revise it once you have heard them. That is how it should go.

If a customer cannot talk this week, keep the conversation booked for week 2. Run the synthesis on the notes you have, and run it again when the rest come in. It rates each finding by how many sources back it, so a thin sample shows.

### Questions for the CEO

1. Three months from now, what would make you say marketing is working? Has anything already been promised?
2. Is there a goal for this quarter, in pipeline, revenue or leads? Who set it?
3. Which numbers do you look at, and where do they come from?
4. Who approves what: budget, customer names, pricing, anything public? How long do they usually take?

Write down what they say, not a tidier version. If there are no goals, do not invent one in the room. Ask for last quarter's numbers, and say you will bring options for them to choose from in week 2. You will build those with [Propose quarterly goals](../skills/budget-and-planning/propose-quarterly-goals.md).

## Exit check

By the end of week 1:

- You have talked to the CEO, and you know their goals, or have said when you will bring options.
- You have talked to the sales lead, two reps and two customers, or those still to come are booked.
- Sales has one output that went through a full draft, review and approval.
- Tracking conventions and draft handoff rules exist, and sales has seen the draft.

By the end of week 4:

- Messaging, ICP and top competitor are written down, and you agree with them.
- Sales has agreed the handoff rules.
- The quarter has goals the CEO chose, a budget and a plan.

## What you can tell the CEO on Friday

You can tell them who you talked to, what you heard, what sales now has and what comes next. Fill this in, and say it or send it:

```
I have talked to <number> of the five people I planned to. The rest are booked for <dates>.
What I heard so far: <up to three findings, and how many people said each>.
Sales now has <the output>.
Tracking names are set, and draft handoff rules are with <sales lead>.
Next week: <top priority>.
By the end of week 4, the quarter will have goals, a budget and a plan.
I need from you: <a decision or a number, or "nothing yet">.
```

Do not say what is working or what the strategy should be. Five conversations cannot show that yet, and it is fine to say so.
