New to this? Run the two-hour starter until your reports, plan and calendar exist, then move to the full routine. Pace, lag and baseline are explained in [the glossary](../office/glossary.md).

## The starter routine

This takes about two hours a week (estimate), on top of your priorities. It does not assume a quarterly plan, a content calendar or CRM reports. Start it in week 2 of [First week](first-week.md), or in week 5 of [Starting from zero](from-zero.md), where the person who sells stands in for sales.

| When | What to do | Time (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Get the numbers you can, by hand or from whoever has them: pipeline, leads, paid spend. Write "none yet" for the rest. A blank is fine, and a guess is not | 30 minutes |
| Monday | [Set weekly priorities](../skills/budget-and-planning/set-weekly-priorities.md), pasting in those numbers, what is due from your playbook, new requests and what sales last said. With no plan, it ranks by dates | 15 minutes |
| A time in the week the sales lead can do | Talk to sales for 30 minutes: which leads were good, which were not, what objections came up, which rivals appeared | 30 minutes |
| Tuesday to Thursday | Work on the top priority, then the others if days allow | The days in your priorities table, not counted in the two hours |
| Tuesday to Thursday | [Review any output](../skills/marketing-ops/review-any-output.md) for anything public or using a customer name or number, then send it to its approver, and publish what comes back approved | 30 minutes |
| Tuesday to Thursday | CEO requests and other interruptions, inside the block you hold for them. See the [interruption rule](#total-time-and-what-to-drop) | 2 hours held, not counted in the two hours |
| Friday | Note what changed: new proof points, a competitor move, a sales objection. If it is a fact, run [Update the context pack](../skills/marketing-ops/update-context-pack.md) | 15 minutes |

Move to the full routine one piece at a time, as each thing comes to exist:

- **Reports built:** step 1 drops to 20 minutes.
- **Goals and a quarterly plan:** paste the plan into step 3, and add step 5.
- **Content calendar:** add step 9.
- **Paid already running:** add step 4 now.

Stay on the starter for anything still missing, for as long as it takes. You have not fallen behind.

## The full routine

The full routine takes about one working day a week, 6 to 9 hours, spread across the days below, plus 2 hours held for CEO requests and other interruptions. That leaves 3.5 to 4 days for your priorities in a 40-hour week, before time off, fixed meetings and delivery you have already committed to. Time estimates are rough, and assume the context pack is filled in and your reports exist.

| Day | Step | Skill or action | Time (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 1 | Pull last week's numbers from your tools: pipeline, leads, paid spend, top pages | 20 minutes, once the reports exist |
| Monday | 2 | Talk to sales, at a time in the week the sales lead can do: which leads were good, which were not, what objections came up, which rivals appeared. If that is not Monday, use what they said last time | 30 minutes |
| Monday | 3 | [Set weekly priorities](../skills/budget-and-planning/set-weekly-priorities.md), pasting in the numbers, this quarter's plan and what sales said | 15 minutes |
| Monday | 4 | [Adjust paid campaigns](../skills/paid-demand-gen/adjust-paid-campaigns.md), if you run paid, pasting in a performance export | 20 minutes |
| Monday | 5 | [Diagnose a pipeline gap](../skills/analytics-and-campaign-evaluation/diagnose-pipeline-gap.md), only where pipeline is rated Behind | 30 minutes |
| Tuesday or Wednesday | 6 | Publish, send and hand over what was approved last week. The office cannot do this for you | 1 hour |
| Tuesday or Wednesday | 7 | Talk to one customer, every two weeks, using your guide from [Write an interview guide](../skills/segmentation-icp-and-personas/write-interview-guide.md). Keep the notes for [Synthesize customer research](../skills/segmentation-icp-and-personas/synthesize-customer-research.md) in [the quarterly playbook](quarterly.md) | 45 minutes, with notes, every two weeks |
| Tuesday to Thursday | 8 | Run the priority work for the week, using the skill it calls for | The days in your priorities table |
| Tuesday to Thursday | 9 | Make next week's items from the calendar you made with [Plan a content calendar](../skills/content/plan-content-calendar.md): [Write a blog post](../skills/content/write-blog-post.md), [Write social posts](../skills/content/write-social-posts.md), [Write a marketing email](../skills/content/write-marketing-email.md) | 2 to 4 hours |
| Tuesday to Thursday | 10 | [Review any output](../skills/marketing-ops/review-any-output.md) for anything public or using a customer name or number | 10 minutes per item |
| Tuesday to Thursday | 11 | Send items to the approvers listed in channels-and-tools.md, so they are back before next Tuesday | 15 minutes |
| Tuesday to Thursday | 12 | CEO requests and other interruptions, inside the block you hold for them | 2 hours, at a fixed time |
| Friday | 13 | Note what changed: new proof points, a competitor move, a sales objection. If it is a fact, run [Update the context pack](../skills/marketing-ops/update-context-pack.md). If you keep [a workboard](../office/my-workboard.md), say `Close my session` and save what comes back | 15 minutes |
| Friday | 14 | Check that approvals are back for next week's publishing | 15 minutes |

**Step 1's 20 minutes is only true once the reports exist.** Until then, pulling numbers by hand takes longer. Build the reports once: run [Specify a dashboard](../skills/analytics-and-campaign-evaluation/specify-dashboard.md), then save one report in your CRM and other tools for each number in step 1, with the date range set to last week. Allow half a day to a day (estimate), with whoever runs your CRM, and treat it as a priority for that week. After that, you only open them.

## Run Monday in one chat

Paste this into a new chat in your project, on either routine. Fill in what you have and write "none yet" for the rest.

```
Act as the head of marketing from the Marketing Office. Follow your charter. Read the context pack. Today's task: run my Monday review from the weekly playbook, in this chat.
Week of: <date>. Days available, counted as in the capacity rule: <number>.
Numbers, last week and to date: <pipeline, MQLs, SQLs, demo requests, paid spend>.
MQLs older than 14 days, and how many became SQLs: <numbers, if you have them>.
Due within two weeks: <items>. Slipped: <items>. New requests: <items>.
What sales last said: <notes>.
Paid export: <paste it, or "no paid">.
Workboard: <paste it, or "none">.
Set weekly priorities first. Adjust paid campaigns only if I pasted an export. Diagnose a pipeline gap only if pipeline is rated Behind. Show the working view. Pause only for decisions on outcome, spending or approval. Spend nothing and mark nothing approved.
```

**You get:** up to three priorities, a do-not-do list, the paid decisions and the pipeline check where they apply, in a short working view. Say `Show the full record` for each skill's full output. **Done means:** you have accepted or changed the priorities, and made any paid changes yourself in the ad tool.

## Total time and what to drop

| Part of the week | Time (estimate) |
|---|---|
| Monday, steps 1 to 4 | About 1.5 hours, plus 30 minutes if pipeline is Behind |
| Publishing and the customer conversation, averaged over two weeks | About 1.5 hours |
| Next week's items, reviews and approvers, assuming three items | 3 to 5 hours |
| Friday | 30 minutes |
| **Routine total** | **About 6 to 9 hours, roughly one working day** |
| CEO requests and other interruptions | 2 hours |

**Capacity rule.** Use one calculation in every plan and calendar:

```
Working days
minus time off and fixed meetings
minus the routine
minus the interruption block
minus delivery you have already committed to
= days available for new work
```

One day is 8 hours. Give the result to [Set weekly priorities](../skills/budget-and-planning/set-weekly-priorities.md) as days available. In a normal week with no time off, no fixed meetings beyond the routine and nothing already committed, that is 3.5 to 4. The routine is already out of that number, so do not count it again. Count each asset once, whether it sits in a campaign plan, the content calendar or a launch plan. Keep contractor days separate, and do not count waiting for approval as work. If you do not know the days or the effort, the answer is Not assessed, not "fits". A week with monthly or quarterly work names what that work replaces.

When the week is over, drop in this order until it fits:

1. Social posts for next week.
2. The blog post for next week.
3. The third priority.
4. The second priority.

Do not drop the 30 minutes with sales, the review and approver steps, or the top priority. A customer conversation can move by one week, and not two in a row.

**Interruption rule.** Hold 2 hours a week, at a fixed time, for CEO requests and other interruptions. That number is a starting rule, not a benchmark. Once you have four weeks of actual numbers, use their average instead. A request goes in this block, not in a priority slot. When requests go over the block, drop from the list above, in the same order, and tell the person asking what moves before you start: "I can have this by Thursday. It moves the search ads review to next week. Is that the right trade?" Only the CEO can move your top priority, and they do it by answering that question. If you go over three weeks in a row, show the CEO the list, and agree a bigger block or fewer priorities.

**One person runs one launch or one major campaign at a time.** If two fall in the same weeks, move one, or shrink one to the small sequence in [the launch playbook](launch.md) or the minimum campaign in [the campaign lifecycle](campaign-lifecycle.md). A small second campaign still takes days, so the combined work must also fit the capacity rule.

## Exit check

- You spoke with sales this week, and with a customer in the last two weeks.
- Up to three priorities were set, and you know whether each got done.
- Everything public went through its approver.
- Next week's items are made, and any new facts are in the context pack.

## If you only have two hours

Run [the starter routine](#the-starter-routine) for this week, whatever week you are in. Skip the rest and say so in next week's input.

## Worked example: a week at CogniATion

This is a fictional illustration. CogniATion is invented, and everything marked illustrative, including this week's numbers, is made up for the example. The goals come from its context pack: $1.2 million in new qualified pipeline and 90 SQLs by March 31, 2027, and the professional services campaign published by February 15.

### Monday: numbers in, priorities out

The marketer pastes illustrative numbers into [Set weekly priorities](../skills/budget-and-planning/set-weekly-priorities.md). Tom Ferrant, head of sales, can only do Thursdays (illustrative), so what sales said comes from last week's 30 minutes.

```
Use the skill "Set weekly priorities". Read the context pack.
Task: set priorities for the week of January 25, 2027.
Pipeline: $70,000 last week, $270,000 to date.
MQLs: 24 last week, 72 to date.
Website demo requests: 4 last week, 16 to date.
Due within two weeks: professional services campaign page and emails,
conference budget request.
Slipped: nothing.
Sales says, last Thursday: no complaints about lead quality this month.
Days available: 4.
```

The draft comes back with this pace check. Pipeline pace is ($1.2 million minus $270,000) divided by 10 weeks left, an estimate.

| KPI | Last week | To date | Target pace | Against target | Baseline pace | Against baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New qualified pipeline | $70,000 | $270,000 | $93,000 | Behind | $69,000 | Above |
| MQLs | 24 | 72 | About 27 | Close | 20 | Above |
| Website demo requests | 4 | 16 | No target | No target | About 4.5 | Level |

The two comparisons answer different questions. Pipeline is behind what this quarter's goal needs, though above a usual Q3 week. MQLs have no goal of their own in the pack, so their target pace works back from the 90 SQL goal and the 26% rate, about 27 a week. Their baseline pace is 260 in Q3 2026 divided by 13 weeks. So 24 beats a usual week and still falls short. Demo requests have no goal to work back from, so they show No target.

And these three priorities:

| # | Priority | Done by Friday means | Owner | Goal served | Days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish professional services campaign page and emails | Drafts ready for the February 15 launch | The marketer | Campaign by February 15 | 2.5 |
| 2 | Find out why pipeline is behind | Search ads export reviewed, likely cause named | The marketer | $1.2 million pipeline | 1 |
| 3 | Send conference budget for approval | Request with Marta Ilves, CEO, who needs 3 days | The marketer | Pipeline, SQLs | 0.5 |

**Feeds the next step:** one Behind week earns a check rather than a fix, so priority 2 starts with a [Diagnose a pipeline gap](../skills/analytics-and-campaign-evaluation/diagnose-pipeline-gap.md) run. The days add up to 4, the top of what the capacity rule allows.

### Monday: the paid decisions

The marketer pastes an export into [Adjust paid campaigns](../skills/paid-demand-gen/adjust-paid-campaigns.md). It judges the rolling four weeks, December 21 to January 17, and leaves out the last seven days, January 18 to 24, because leads take time to qualify. All of it is illustrative, including the $234 target cost per MQL: paid's $27,000 for the quarter divided by 30 expected SQLs, times the 26% MQL to SQL rate.

| Line | Spend | MQLs | Cost per MQL | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Search, critical thinking terms | $3,780 | 21 | $180 | Keep | Below the $234 target |
| Search, general AI training terms | $820 | 0 | None | Cut | Over $702, three times the target, with no MQLs |
| Social, heads of learning | $3,600 | 8 | $450 | Change one thing | Above the $351 limit, 1.5 times the target. Limit the audience to professional services firms |

The cut money, about $205 a week, moves to the critical thinking terms. The search total does not change, so no approval is needed. The marketer makes these changes in the ad tool, since the office cannot.

**Feeds the next step:** the same export and the MQL counts go into the pipeline check.

### Monday: the pipeline check

The marketer pastes in illustrative numbers from the CRM: 20 SQLs from 72 MQLs so far, at about $13,500 each, which matches the $270,000 to date. Pipeline is SQLs times deal size, so there are three places the gap can be:

- **Are deals smaller?** No. $13,500 matches the average in goals-and-kpis.md.
- **Are fewer MQLs becoming SQLs?** No. Conversion is 28% against the 26% baseline.
- **Are there too few MQLs?** Yes. The remaining $930,000 needs about 69 more SQLs at $13,500, or 7 a week. At 26%, that is about 27 MQLs a week, against 24 last week. These are estimates.

So both readings are true: 24 MQLs beats Q3's usual 20, and falls short of the 27 this bigger goal needs. This shows where the gap is, not why. Finding out why is the search ads review in priority 2.

**Feeds the next step:** the marketer now knows why the campaign date matters. The campaign is the added volume, so priority 1 stays first. Nothing else gets added.

### Tuesday: publish and make

Last week's approved items go out on Tuesday. From Tuesday to Thursday, priority 1 runs through [Write a web page](../skills/content/write-web-page.md) and [Write a marketing email](../skills/content/write-marketing-email.md), then [Review any output](../skills/marketing-ops/review-any-output.md). They run in one chat, so each one reads the brief. Priority 3 goes to Marta Ilves on Tuesday, leaving her 3 days, so it is done. No customer conversation is due this week.

### Wednesday: the CEO request pushes out a priority

Marta Ilves asks for a one-page marketing update for an investor call on Thursday. The request and its timing are illustrative. It will take about 8 hours (estimate), and the block holds 2, so the week is 6 hours short.

The marketer goes down the drop list. Next week's social posts save about 1 hour. No blog post is due, and priority 3 is already done. Priority 2 is next, and its day covers the other 5 hours, so the search ads review moves. Before starting, the marketer tells Marta:

> I can have this by Thursday noon. It moves the search ads review to next week, so by Friday I will know pipeline is short on volume, but not why. Is that the right trade?

She says yes (illustrative). The update goes through [Review any output](../skills/marketing-ops/review-any-output.md), and it says what the marketer knows: pipeline is behind pace and short on volume, and the reason is not yet known. It does not soften that because investors will read it.

### Friday: what slipped, and a new objection

The 30 minutes with Tom Ferrant still happened on Thursday, since it is not on the drop list. Priority 1 is done, and priority 3 is with Marta. Priority 2 failed its test for done, and the marketer records that rather than calling it nearly done. Next Monday's paste will say "Slipped: search ads export review". If it slips again, Set weekly priorities makes the marketer do it that week or drop it.

Also on Friday, Tom Ferrant passes on something a managing partner asked on a first call. The objection is illustrative. The marketer adds it to personas.md, under persona 3, with its source:

```
Objections
- "Why not just tell people to check the AI's work?"
- "Can I see how each associate scored?" (Reported by Tom Ferrant
  from a first call, January 29, 2027.)
```

**Feeds next week:** [Write a web page](../skills/content/write-web-page.md) reads objections from personas.md, so the campaign page can answer this one with a fact already in product.md: learning leaders see progress by team, and never see an individual's conversation. A buyer who wants scores for performance reviews meets a disqualifier in icp-and-segments.md, so the marketer also tells sales. Next Monday's paste starts from where this week ended.

The same campaign runs through every stage in [the campaign lifecycle example](campaign-lifecycle.md#worked-example-the-february-campaign).
